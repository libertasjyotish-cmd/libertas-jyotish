Marriage timing and compatibility in Vedic astrology

Vedic astrology has traditionally been used to consider marriage, and in India charts are still compared when a match is proposed. Here we explain which factors marriage is read from and how the timing is worked out, unpacking the technical terms as we go.

Marriage is read from the 7th house

The birth chart is divided into twelve houses, each responsible for an area of life. Marriage and the spouse are shown by the 7th house. Which sign falls there, where its ruler is placed, and which planets occupy it: from these we read what you look for in a partner and how you tend to build a relationship. In the classical approach, Jupiter in a woman's chart and Venus in a man's chart are also used as indicators of the spouse.

D9 (Navamsha): the second chart

Vedic astrology has divisional charts, which subdivide the birth chart further. The one always consulted for marriage is D9, the Navamsha, a second chart made by dividing each sign of the birth chart into nine parts. The surface nature appears in the birth chart; the essence that emerges within married life is said to appear in D9, and the two are read together. A planet that is unremarkable in the birth chart may become strong in D9, which is read as marriage changing the direction of a person's life.

Timing is read from Dasha and transits

"When will I marry?" is judged by layering the Dasha (planetary periods) with the current movement of the planets (transits). Are you in the period of a planet connected to the 7th house, its ruler, Venus or Jupiter? Is Jupiter or Saturn actually passing through the 7th house at the time? When these two coincide, matters of partnership tend to move. Put the other way, rushing when the conditions are not in place tends to be draining. Note that this indicates periods of higher likelihood; it does not settle whether or when a marriage will happen.

How compatibility between two people is read

The method long used in India is Ashtakoota, which compares the two people's lunar mansions (Nakshatras) on eight points and gives a score out of 36, with 18 or more generally taken as the benchmark. The score is only the entry point, though. In practice, more weight is given to overlaying the two charts and seeing which of your areas the other person's planets fall into (the 7th, 2nd, 8th houses and so on). Relationships with low scores last; relationships with high scores fail to mesh. Compatibility is most useful not as a verdict of "suited or not" but as a map of where you support each other and where friction is likely.
