An astrology of the hand, born in India three thousand years ago

Hasta Samudrika Shastra — the source of palmistry

Most of the world’s palmistry traces its source to India. Indian palmistry was never an independent “palm reading”: it was born from the same root as Jyotish, as the science of reading the signs of the stars as they appear on the body. The classical Samudrika Shastra assigns the mounts, lines and marks of the palm to the nine planets (Navagraha) and reads the hand as a “horoscope you carry with you”. That is why palmistry and the birth chart were never two separate arts, but one system reading the same nine planets in Heaven and on Earth.

Why it has to be both

A reading from the birth chart alone tells you the blueprint you were handed at birth — but not how much of it you are actually living. A reading from the hands alone shows your present position — but has no yardstick to judge whether that position is your true ground or a detour. Only when Heaven and Earth are compared can we read whether your hands have already received what the stars promised, which doors are still unopened, and which way you should turn. The premise of this science: a planet strong in the birth chart leaves a mark in the same place on the hand. Where they agree, “the hand has already received what the stars promised.” Where they differ, “there is a gift not yet received.”

Left and right — two times, three karmas

The non-dominant hand shows what you were born with (innate); the dominant hand shows what you have cultivated in this life (acquired). Indian philosophy speaks of karma in three layers.

SanchitaThe store the soul brought with it. Seen in the whole birth chart and in the non-dominant hand.
PrarabdhaThe portion destined to be experienced in this life. Appears as the sequence of dashas (planetary periods).
KriyamanaThe karma you are making right now. Appears as changes in the lines of the dominant hand.

The birth chart is the script the soul received; the non-dominant hand is a copy of that script; the dominant hand is the stage you are performing on today. This report reads all three side by side.

Auspicious marks (Lakshana) — sacred signs hidden in the hand

What sets Indian palmistry decisively apart is that it reads marks, not just lines: the fish (Matsya), conch (Shankha), lotus (Padma), trident (Trishula), wheel (Chakra), flag (Dhvaja), temple (Mandir)… In the classics, when the shape of a deity’s emblem appears on the hand, that deity’s protection is said to be present. Which marks are on your hands, and which planet or nakshatra deity in your chart they resonate with — this is the most thrilling chapter.

FAQ

Q. How is this different from a palm-reading app?
An app returns generic text from a single photo. This report compares your birth chart and both hands in the language of the same nine planets, and writes the gap between blueprint and present, the direction to take, the three layers of karma, the auspicious marks and the dasha timing as one story. As far as we know, no other report integrates palm and birth chart in one volume written this individually. And you can follow the changes by re-photographing.
Q. What if the palm and the chart say different things?
That is the heart of this reading. The chart is the blueprint you were given; the hand is where you stand now. A difference is not a flaw: it is read as power not yet used up, or a door you opened yourself, and from it we derive the direction to take (Chapter 4).
Q. What if I have no auspicious marks?
Value is not decided by marks. Forming crossings and characteristic lines are read by the same method, together with their resonance with your chart.
Q. I don’t know my birth time.

Q. Why both hands?
The non-dominant hand shows what you carried; the dominant hand shows what you are performing now. One hand cannot show the three layers of karma.
Q. Will it say bad things?
Health and lifespan are not covered. Hard periods always come with the reason and with what is waiting beyond them.
Q. How long are photos kept?
Originals are deleted automatically after 30 days. Only analysis data and low-resolution comparison images are kept.
